Recommended ESPs

https://help.kit.com/en → How to Set Up Your Email Service Provider With Kit →

Kit is an email service provider designed for creators. It allows you to manage your email list, create automated sequences, and send broadcasts to your audience. Setting up Kit involves connecting your webpages, importing contacts, and creating your first email sequence.

How to Send a Broadcast Email in Kit →

Broadcast emails allow you to send a single email to your entire list or a specific segment. This feature is perfect for announcements, promotions, or any timely content you want to share. To send a broadcast, you'll choose your recipients, craft your message, and schedule or send it immediately.

How to Set Up an Automated Email Sequence/Funnel in Kit →

An automated email sequence, or funnel, allows you to send a series of pre-scheduled emails to your subscribers based on their actions or specific timing. This is ideal for nurturing leads, onboarding new subscribers, or delivering a course. Setting up a sequence involves creating your emails, setting triggers, and determining the timing of each message.

How to Create Segments and Apply Tags in Kit →

Creating segments and applying tags helps you organize your email list by grouping subscribers based on their interests, behaviors, or other criteria. Segments allow you to target specific audiences with tailored content, while tags help you track and manage subscriber actions.

KAJABI

How to Set Up Your Email Service Provider With Kajabi →

Kajabi is an all-in-one platform that lets you manage your email marketing alongside your courses, website, and other digital products. Setting up your email service provider in Kajabi starts with setting up your Marketing information and involves configuring your email settings.

How to Send a Broadcast Email in Kajabi →

Sending a broadcast email in Kajabi allows you to reach your entire email list or specific segments with one-time messages like promotions, updates, or newsletters. You can customize your email content, select your recipients, and either schedule the email or send it right away.

How to Set Up an Automated Email Sequence/Funnel in Kajabi →

An automated email sequence, or funnel, allows you to nurture your audience by sending a series of emails triggered by specific actions or time intervals. This is perfect for onboarding new subscribers, delivering a course, or guiding leads through your sales process. Setting up a sequence involves creating your emails, defining triggers, and scheduling the delivery.

How to Create Segments and Apply Tags in Kajabi

Creating segments and applying tags helps you organize your audience based on their behaviors, preferences, or actions. Segments allow you to target specific groups with personalized content, while tags help you track and manage subscriber interactions.

A lead magnet is a free resource that solves a specific problem for your audience, such as a guide, checklist, cheat sheet, audio, or video training.

Begin with Step 1 to create your lead magnet. Then move to Step 2 to set up your opt-in page, thank you page, and connect your Email Service Provider (ESP).

Once your lead magnet is created, it’s time to set up three key pieces:

An opt-in page (to collect emails)

A thank-you page (to confirm sign-up)

A delivery email (to send your lead magnet)

You have two main setup options:

Option 1: Use an All-in-One Platform

Kit and Kajabi both let you build your opt-in page, thank-you page, and email delivery sequence all in one place. Each platform includes ready-to-use templates, so you can quickly customize your setup without starting from scratch. This is the fastest and most beginner-friendly option because everything lives inside a single platform.

Upload or link your lead magnet so it’s automatically delivered to new subscribers

For KAJABI

Step 1: How to Set Up an Opt-In Page →

An opt-in form in Kajabi is essential for capturing leads by encouraging visitors to subscribe to your offers. To set up an opt-in form, you’ll select a template, customize it with your branding and offer details, and configure the fields to collect subscriber information.

Step 2: How to Set Up a Thank You/Confirmation Page →

A Thank You/Confirmation page in Kajabi helps acknowledge and confirm actions such as signing up for an offer or making a purchase. To create one, select a template, personalize it with your message and branding, and configure it to appear after the visitor completes their action.

Step 3: How to Deliver Your Lead Magnet →

To deliver your lead magnet efficiently, you need to set up an automated sequence in Kajabi. This involves creating an email sequence that automatically sends your lead magnet to new subscribers when they sign up. You'll configure Kajabi to deliver the lead magnet and schedule any follow-up emails you want to include.

Option 2: Use DCA Templates with Showit or Canva

If you prefer more design flexibility, you can use our DCA templates with Showit or Canva to create your opt-in and thank-you pages. You’ll then connect those pages to your email platform (like Kit or Kajabi) to deliver your lead magnet.

Creating a Workshop Course

Q: Does a Workshop Course really need to be 1 hour? Can it be shorter? Longer?

A: A Workshop Course can definitely be shorter or longer than 1 hour. The important thing is to make sure you're delivering your content in a way that will best provide your students with the instant clarity and results your workshop promises. If you can get that done in 1 hour, that is fine; if it takes longer than 1 hour, that is fine, too.

If your workshop course exceeds 2 hours, consider simplifying some steps. Remember, participants are looking for transformation, not an overload of information. Alternatively, you could remove some content and offer it as a PDF download or in a workbook. Since you're delivering this live, it's crucial to ensure that people can stay engaged throughout the entire session!

One thing to keep in mind if you decide to have a workshop course that runs less than 1 hour is that the perceived value may go down. If you don't have enough content for the last 20-30 minutes to make it a 1-hour workshop, then add on a great 20-30 minute Q&A at the end. If you know you won't have a bunch of questions coming in Live, then prepare several in advance that you know your ideal customer might have and start with those.

Q: Is it too much to plan a workshop course while also creating a larger full course?

Q: How do I choose a Workshop Course topic for the Start From Scratch track without overlapping or competing with the full course I'll create later in the Ready to Launch track?

A: Ask yourself this key question: What is something that would be genuinely valuable to your ideal customer, give them a quick win, and move them closer to being ready for your future digital course?

There are three strategic ways to approach this:

Option 1: Choose a topic that covers beginner-level content your ideal customer would need before they’re ready for your full course. This approach helps your audience build a foundation and positions them to be more successful when they enroll in your signature program. For example, if your future course teaches how to stitch an advanced fall-themed table runner, your Workshop Course could focus on embroidery basics like transferring a design, threading a needle, and learning a few key stitches. By the time they finish your workshop, they’ll be more confident and ready for your advanced course.

Option 2: Choose a topic that represents a small slice of what you’ll eventually teach, delivered in a way that offers a clear, actionable result. For example, if your future course teaches hairstylists how to open and run a successful salon, your Workshop Course could focus on one strategy like using Facebook ads to get 10 new clients this month. It gives them a quick win, builds momentum, and makes them more qualified for your full course where you’ll go deeper into systems, hiring, finances, and other salon operations.

Option 3: Choose a topic that attracts the same type of person who would benefit from your future course, even if it’s unrelated to the actual course content. The value is in drawing in your ideal customer, building trust, and offering something useful. For example, I once taught a workshop called “How to Plan Your 2023 Promo Calendar.” While that wasn’t the core content of List Builders Society, it appealed to the same audience and gave them a useful tool while preparing them to take the next step with me.

No matter which option you choose, your goal is to create something helpful and results-driven that naturally leads your audience closer to the bigger transformation you’ll offer in your full course.

Q: Should I offer more than one date and time for my Workshop Course like people do for a webinar?

A: I recommend sticking with just one date and time. The goal of the Workshop Course is to keep things simple, scrappy, and low-tech so you can get your offer out there, validate your idea, and start building momentum.

Offering a single time slot reduces complexity for you and removes decision fatigue for your audience. It also creates urgency and encourages people to commit. Once you’ve delivered it Live, you can always make a replay available if you’d like to extend the offer window.

Q: Should I include a workbook to accompany the Live training of my Workshop Course?

A: A workbook can be a great way to increase engagement and help your students take action during your Live Workshop Course, but it’s not required. If you’re going to create one, make sure you give your students access to it before the workshop so they can print it out and come prepared.

I recommend linking it in your registration confirmation email and your reminder emails leading up to the Live session. You don’t want to wait and drop it during the workshop itself because that can distract people right when you want them focused.

If a workbook feels overwhelming, skip it. You can still offer something helpful by turning your slide deck into a simple PDF and sharing it in your post-workshop email along with the replay link.

And if you do create a workbook, keep it simple. Canva and Google Docs both work great. The most important thing is that it supports your teaching without slowing you down.

Q: Should I offer refunds for my Workshop Course, and how should I handle refund requests?

A: I recommend offering a 48-hour refund window that starts once your Workshop Course goes Live. This gives people the chance to try it out while also removing the risk, which can help increase sales.

You can use simple language like this on your mini sales page: “If you’re not completely satisfied with this training, you have 48 hours after the workshop training date to request a refund. No questions asked.” Include an email address or clear instructions for how to submit a request.

If you decide not to offer refunds, that’s perfectly fine too. You’re running a business and you get to set the rules. Just make sure your policy is clear from the start and have a prepared response ready that is kind but firm. If someone still reaches out, you can always ask for feedback to understand their experience. And if you have the time and energy, you might offer a quick call or even a short coaching session in exchange for their insights. Feedback is always valuable, even when it comes from someone who didn’t love your training.

Q: How do I figure out my webinar show-up rate and conversion rates?

A: Tracking your webinar performance is essential, and understanding how to calculate your show-up and conversion rates will help you know what’s working and where to improve. Here’s how to calculate each one:

Webinar Show-Up Rate (percent of registrants who attended Live):

Use this formula:

(Number of people who attended the Live webinar ÷ Number of people who registered) × 100

For example, if 200 people registered and 50 showed up Live:

(50 ÷ 200) × 100 = 25% show-up rate

Tip: Your webinar platform (like Demio or Zoom) will show you the number of Live attendees under the "attendees" section.

Live Webinar Conversion Rate (percent of Live attendees who purchased):

Use this formula:

(Number of units sold during the Live webinar ÷ Number of Live attendees) × 100

For example, if 50 people attended Live and 5 purchased during the webinar:

(5 ÷ 50) × 100 = 10% conversion rate

Tip: To find the total number of webinar registrants, go into your CRM and click on the tag you created for your webinar registrations. That will show you everyone who signed up.

Tracking these numbers gives you a clear picture of how effective your webinar is at both getting people to show up and converting them into students. You can use this data to adjust your registration emails, your webinar content, and your offer for stronger results next time.

I personally tend to lean toward “workshop” when I’m speaking to a broader or newer audience, because it’s approachable and action-oriented. But if you’re teaching something with a little more polish or depth, “masterclass” might elevate the perception of the event. It’s all about how you want the experience to feel, and what your audience will respond to most.

Test it out and see what feels right to you. There’s no wrong answer as long as it’s clear, compelling, and gets people to show up.

There’s no one right answer. It’s about how you want to run your business and show up for your audience over the long term.

Q: If I decide to trademark the name of my course, should I trademark it internationally or just in the country in which I am based?

A: Trademark rights are territorial, which means they’re granted on a country-by-country basis. There is no such thing as a “global” trademark. Hence, you have to file a trademark application in each country (or region, such as the European Union, in some cases) where you seek legal protection.

Determining where to file for trademark protection is largely a business decision. You should only file in countries that are commercially significant to your success.

Q: The video files when recording my course are huge. How can I reduce the file size?

A: Here is an answer from our tech guru himself, Dave Kaminski:

Video files are big, always. A 45-minute video, assuming it's HD (1920x1080), will typically be anywhere from 2.5GB to 5GB in size. That's just the nature of video files.

This is not a "Screenflow problem.” All video, produced from any video editing software, works the same way.

To reduce the file size of a video, you have to reduce the quality of the video. That means the video file size will be smaller, but it won't look as good.

There are a couple of ways to do this… or you can do both together.

First, you can reduce the "bit rate" or "data rate" of the video. By cutting this number in half, you'll typically cut the file size of the video in half… but again, the video quality will take a hit (and sometimes a major one).

Second, you can reduce the dimensions of the video. Instead of exporting a video at 1920x1080, export it at 1280x720. In this case, you're making the video smaller, which helps to reduce file size. You generally won't notice a difference in quality unless you watch the video at full screen.

But the bottom line is whether you're using Screenflow, Camtasia, Adobe Premiere, iMovie, or whatever... video file sizes will always be the same... and you have to reduce the quality of the video to get smaller file sizes. And even then, you're going to have big files.

Q: What is the difference between a course transformation statement and a "hook"?

A: Your Course Transformation Statement is all about the results or transformation your students will walk away with after completing your course. In this course, you will (TRANSFORMATION) in (TIMEFRAME) to (ADDRESS PAIN POINTS USING MOVING AWAY FROM LANGUAGE).

On the other hand, your hook is part of your marketing message. It’s the angle you use to draw people in and capture their attention. Your hook should make people curious and position your course as uniquely valuable. It often focuses on something surprising, counterintuitive, emotionally compelling, or ultra-relevant. You’ll use your hook throughout your messaging, especially on your sales page. (You’ll find more guidance on this inside the Sales Page Blueprint in Module 5, Lesson 3.)

Here are a few quick examples to show the difference:

Let’s say you’re a home organizer and your course teaches people how to declutter their homes.

Your Course Transformation Statement might be: “In this course, you will go from feeling overwhelmed to feeling organized and experience a clean and clutter-free home.”

Your hook could be: Organization doesn’t have to be hard. It’s actually easier than living in chaos.

If you're a yoga instructor teaching pregnant women how to safely practice yoga:

Your Course Transformation Statement might be: “In this course, you will learn how to incorporate safe yoga poses for each pregnancy stage into your daily routine.”

Your hook could be: You don’t have to stop exercising during pregnancy. A safe yoga routine can help you feel better now and even help you in the delivery room.

If you're a personal stylist teaching how to create new outfits from clothes people already own:

Your Course Transformation Statement might be: “In this course, you will go from experiencing daily decision fatigue with your closet options to feeling clear and excited about what to wear and what not to wear from the items in your own closet.”

Your hook could be: You don’t need to buy more clothes or hire a stylist. Your next favorite outfit is already in your closet.

Think of it this way: your transformation statement sells the outcome. Your hook gets them to stop and pay attention long enough to care about the outcome. Both are essential, and when they work together, they make your course stand out in a big way.

Having a clear ICA makes every part of your marketing easier. It guides how you write your emails, what you say on social media, the examples you use when you teach, and even how you structure your offers. Instead of guessing what to create or how to say it, you can come back to your ICA and ask, “Would this help them? Would this resonate with them?” This helps you attract students or customers who are excited to work with you and who are most likely to get results from what you offer.

The clearer you are on your ICA, the more confident and consistent you will feel in your business. It becomes the foundation for your messaging, your content, and the products you create.

Q: What do I do if my course solves a problem that is time-sensitive, like for pregnant women, but I won't be launching until after their window of need has passed?

A: When your ICA has a time-sensitive need, it can be tempting to skip straight to an evergreen course. But I still recommend doing at least one Live launch first. In fact, I usually suggest two or three, but in this case, one solid Live launch can give you the momentum and insight you need to move faster into evergreen.

The truth is, you may miss the window for a few ideal customers in your first round. But that is OK. You can still use their input, questions, and feedback to shape and refine your course so that it is even more powerful for the next group of students in that same situation.Launching Live gives you something evergreen cannot: real-time interaction. You get to hear directly from your audience, learn what resonates, answer objections, and improve both your course and your marketing as you go. That kind of feedback is gold when you are preparing for an evergreen funnel.

So even with a time-sensitive audience, resist the urge to rush. Do a Live launch first, pay close attention to what your ICA says and needs, and then transition into evergreen with a much stronger and more effective course.

Q: How do I address inclusion in my course when I've been very specific about my ICA? For example, my course is written for moms, but dads might also find it helpful.

A: The more specific you can get with your ICA, the better. That clarity helps you speak directly to the person you most want to serve and allows your content and marketing to connect more deeply. But being specific does not mean being exclusive.

For example, I often speak directly to women and refer to my students as “she,” but I still have amazing men in my audience. When I know there are others listening in who don’t perfectly match my ICA, I try to acknowledge them. A simple phrase like, “Even though I’m speaking directly to moms here, if you're a dad, this will absolutely apply to you too,” can go a long way.

You do not need to water down your messaging. Keep your focus tight, but be thoughtful in how you communicate. Acknowledge who you’re primarily speaking to, and make space for others who may benefit as well. That balance allows you to stay true to your ICA while still being welcoming and inclusive.

Q: How long after a Workshop Course launch should I wait before launching my full digital course?

A: I typically recommend leaving about 2 to 3 months between big launch offers. That gives your audience time to implement what they learned in your Workshop Course and get results, which builds trust and primes them for your next offer. But the right timeline really depends on your audience and how your offers complement each other.

If your full course is the next logical step and your students are ready, you can absolutely offer it sooner. I’ve seen students transition directly from their Workshop Course into a full course launch with success. Some even invite students to enroll during the Q&A portion of their Live workshop by saying something like, “If you’re ready to go deeper with this, my full course is the next step.”

Just be sure you’re not rushing. Ask yourself if your audience has had enough time to see progress and if you’ve given them enough support to build trust and momentum.

Also, make space in your calendar to serve. The most successful launches I’ve had are the ones where I showed up consistently with free, valuable content in the weeks leading up to the promo. It’s what builds the relationship and makes your audience eager to take the next step with you.

Q: How can I design my course to meet the needs of clients at different levels of experience?

A: Start by getting clear on who you most want to serve. Put a stake in the ground and decide which level of experience you’re designing your course for. I always recommend focusing on the largest segment of your audience or the group that feels like the low-hanging fruit.

Once you’ve chosen your core audience, you can support others through strategic bonuses and resources. For example, if your course is aimed at intermediate students, you could offer a “Start From Scratch” bonus for beginners who need help getting up to speed. On the flip side, if you have some advanced strategies that go beyond the core course, those can be offered as a bonus or optional module for more experienced students.

The key is to make your main course content focused and clear for one experience level. Then, use bonuses, a resource library, or weekly support emails to bridge any gaps. In your video lessons, you can guide your students by saying things like, “If you need more help with this step, check out the bonus training below.”

You don’t have to create a course that tries to serve everyone equally. Serve one group well, and support the others with thoughtful extras. That’s how you create a streamlined, high-value experience without overwhelming your students or yourself.

Q: What should I do if someone is already selling a course like the one I want to create?

A: Chances are, someone out there has already created a course similar to the one you want to build. That’s totally normal. There are plenty of courses on list building, digital course creation, and webinar launches, which are all topics I teach. I don’t let that stop me, and I don’t want it to stop you either.

What sets your course apart is you. Your experience, your voice, your style, and your approach are what make your course unique. There is always room in the market when you bring your own perspective and serve your audience with heart.

It’s fine to be aware of what others are offering, but don’t let it distract you. The more time you spend watching what other people are doing, the more likely you are to fall into comparison. That’s not helpful for your creativity or your confidence. Keep your focus on serving your audience and delivering value in your own way. That’s what will help you stand out.

Q: How do I reconcile pricing for B2B vs. B2C customers, when companies usually pay less per person than individuals would?

A: When you’re selling to a business instead of an individual, it makes sense to create a licensing package. This allows a company to buy a set number of licenses for a flat fee. You’ll need to find a pricing structure that makes sense for your business and still allows you to hit your revenue goals.

The key is to think in terms of volume and value. While the per-person price may be lower than what you would charge a single consumer, the total sale amount is often higher. You can also build in bonus elements that make the offer more attractive to a team or business. For example, you might include a Live training session or an extra support package just for their team. This is something you likely would not offer to individual students, but it adds value in a group setting.

Focus on packaging your course in a way that meets the needs of the business while still protecting the value of what you’re offering. It’s not about underpricing for volume. It’s about structuring the deal in a way that works for everyone.

Q: My ICA is not ready for the course I planned to create. What should I do?

A: This is actually more common than you might think. A lot of times, when we’re validating our course ideas, we discover we’ve been creating the course we want to teach for the people we want to work with, but those people might not be our actual audience. The good news is, there’s no right or wrong answer here. You get to decide how to move forward.

Here are a few options to consider:

Stick with the course you want to create. If this is the path you choose, then start building a new audience that is more aligned with your course. Create content that speaks to this new ICA, and start a fresh list where you can tag them appropriately. That’s who you’ll be marketing your course to when it’s time to launch.

Create a workshop course first. If your audience isn’t quite ready for your full course, consider starting with a workshop course. Inside the Your Start From Scratch Digital Course Track, I walk you through how to create a workshop that delivers what I call a “first step transformation.” You help your audience take the very first step toward the bigger result your full course will eventually deliver. This is a great way to meet your audience where they are and get them into action.

Use your pre-launch and off-season to prepare your audience. This is a great time to share free content that teaches the foundational skills your students will need in order to succeed in your course. For example, if your course teaches how to build a membership site, you might use your free content to teach the basics of community-building or content planning. That way, when it’s time to promote your paid offer, your audience already has the context and motivation to take the next step with you.

This is your business and your course. Whether you shift your content, your audience, or both, you’ve got options, and clarity is just the first step. Keep going. You’ve got this.

Q: How do we approach course creation and talking to our ICA when the desire, rather than the pain point, is at the forefront?

A: It’s the same foundational process, but instead of focusing on what they want to get away from, you focus on what they’re moving toward. Start by getting really clear on their desire. Why do they want it? What do they believe will change in their life or business when they get it? What does success look like to them?

During your ICA conversations, pay attention to how they talk about that desire. Are they using words like freedom, confidence, flexibility, or growth? The language they use is important. You’ll want to reflect that back to them in your messaging and course content.

When it comes to your marketing, your job is to paint a clear and compelling picture of what life can look like once that desire is met. Help them imagine the transformation. Use storytelling, examples, and testimonials to show what’s possible. Your course becomes the bridge between where they are now and where they want to go.

Q: How can I create my Course Transformation Statement if I can't guarantee something or it doesn't have any quantitative measurables?

A: If your course doesn’t offer a specific or measurable result, that’s okay. You can still create a strong Course Transformation Statement by focusing on the tools, strategies, and guidance your students will walk away with. What you can promise is that your course will provide the exact framework or steps they need to work toward the transformation they want.

When you can't guarantee a measurable result, speak to how they will feel, what they’ll be able to do, or how they’ll think differently after going through your course. Words like clarity, confidence, understanding, structure, and motivation are powerful here and can point to a meaningful before-and-after transformation.

If you’ve worked with clients or students in any way before, share stories and testimonials that illustrate what’s possible. That way, even if your promise is more general, you’re still showing tangible outcomes. You’re not guaranteeing those outcomes, but you’re demonstrating what is realistic based on past experience.

Your Course Transformation Statement doesn’t have to include numbers to be effective. It just has to be clear, relevant, and aligned with what your ideal customer wants most.

Q: Is it better to launch your course more than once, or to take advantage of the momentum and launch your second course?

A: I always teach my students to launch their first course two or three times before ever creating a second course. That approach has been the foundation of my own business success, and it’s what I’ve seen work best for so many of my peers and students.

Each time you launch the same course, everything gets better. Your webinar gets stronger, your marketing becomes more effective, your email copy improves, and once you have student feedback, you can even improve the course content itself. Every launch builds on the one before it.

If you move on to a second course too quickly, you’re leaving opportunity on the table. Especially if you were planning to sell that second course to the same audience, you’re limited to the smaller list you built from just one launch. But if you launch your first course a second or third time, you expand your audience and deepen your credibility.

Then, when you are ready to introduce a second course, you’ll have a bigger, more engaged email list and a proven track record with your students. That momentum will make your next offer even more successful.

Q: How do I create content if my ICA for the sale is different from my ICA for the course (i.e. Parent/Child, Business Owner/Employee, etc.)?

A: This all comes down to validation. You need to talk to both audiences. Yes, that means doubling up on your course calls, but it is absolutely worth the extra effort.

Start by getting on the phone with the person who will be buying the course. You need to understand what results they are looking for, what would make them feel confident investing in your program, and how they measure success. Their perspective will help you shape your marketing message, sales page, and overall offer.

Next, validate with the person who will actually be going through your course. This is just as important, because the content needs to meet their needs and help them get real results. Ask what they already know, what they are struggling with, how they prefer to learn, and what would make them feel supported and successful inside the course.

Sometimes the buyer doesn't fully understand what the participant needs in order to get results, so it is your job to make sure your content delivers for both sides. Once you gather those insights, you will be able to build a course that supports the learner while still giving the buyer confidence that their investment will pay off.

When you take the time to understand both audiences, you can build a course that creates results and stands out as a well-rounded solution.

Q: Can I send my course validation questions to my ICA in advance via email to give them the chance to prepare before our call?

A: Yes, in most cases, sending your questions ahead of time is totally fine. Thoughtful answers can be really valuable, and giving your ICA time to prepare might help them reflect and give you more in-depth responses.

That said, if your topic is especially personal or emotionally charged and you want more raw, honest answers, it’s often better to ask the questions in real time. Sometimes the most powerful insights come when someone is speaking from the heart without overthinking it.

Try sending your questions in advance for a few calls and see how it feels. If your ICA shows up prepared and open, great. If they seem overly rehearsed or guarded, you can always adjust your approach. This process is meant to be flexible so you can find what works best for both you and your audience.

Q: During my validation calls, I discovered two very different ICA groups that both need my knowledge. Do I do two tracks in one course, or should I create two different courses?

A: This is what I like to call a high-quality problem. I ran into the same situation when I was building Digital Course Academy. I had one group of entrepreneurs who already had established email lists and marketing experience, and another group who were just starting out online. They both wanted to create a digital course, but they were in completely different places.

In my case, I created two tracks inside DCA to support both groups. If your course content can serve both audiences and you’re able to create two clear, focused tracks, then go for it. Just know that creating two tracks is a more advanced move. It takes extra time and planning to get it right.

If you find that each group needs very different messaging, examples, or even outcomes, it might make more sense to create two separate courses. But don’t do that all at once. Start with the ICA that feels easiest or most aligned with your current business and focus on getting that course out the door and launched successfully. Then come back and create the second course when you’re ready.

Another option is to keep the course content the same and speak to each ICA differently in your marketing. You could create two separate webinars that both lead into the same course. I’ve had students do this in industries like yoga, where both yoga teachers and yoga students could benefit from the course, but they needed to hear different messages in order to see the value. The offer was the same, but the path to get there just looked a little different for each group.

Bottom line: start with one course, and one ICA. Get it launched and refined. Then you’ll be in a much better position to decide how to serve the other audience.

Q: What do you do when your audience really isn't sharing during your validation calls? Is this a red flag that it's not a viable niche?

A: Not necessarily. Just because someone isn’t opening up right away doesn’t mean your niche isn’t viable. Sometimes people feel a little nervous getting on a call with someone they don’t know, or they’re unsure what kind of input you’re looking for.

Try reframing how you introduce the call. Let them know you’re creating something new and want to understand what they truly need. Keep the focus on them. You might say something like, “I’m creating a brand-new program and would love to understand more about where you’re at in your journey. If you're open to it, I’d love to ask a few questions. It won’t take much of your time.” That kind of invitation feels more personal and less like an interview.

Also, remember to listen more than you speak. If there’s a pause after they respond, resist the urge to jump in right away. A brief pause gives them space to reflect and often leads to deeper, more honest sharing. This little bit of silence can be incredibly powerful, as people will often keep talking just to fill the gap.

If you're still not getting much engagement, consider offering a small incentive for their time. It could be a 15-minute coaching session, a strategy call, a $10 gift card, or access to a workshop. A simple gesture can go a long way in encouraging participation and showing that you value their input.

The most important thing is to keep showing up and being curious. If you're not getting the insights you need after several calls, it may be a sign to tweak your questions or approach, not necessarily your niche.

What to Do After Your First Launch

Q: How long should I wait until I launch the same program again?

A: I recommend giving yourself at least three months before launching the same program again. This allows time for you to not only deliver the program you just sold but also create space to focus on nurturing your audience and building your email list in between launches, which is a huge factor in increasing sales the next time around.

If you're planning on launching the same program twice a year, a great schedule to follow is one launch in January or February, and a second one in the fall, around September or October. That cadence tends to work really well because it gives you the entire summer to implement a strong pre-launch runway after your first launch wraps up.

Of course, the best timing ultimately depends on your niche and your ideal customer’s buying patterns. Some niches may perform better during different times of the year, so pay attention to what’s happening in your space and adjust accordingly. Just be sure you're giving yourself and your audience enough breathing room to stay excited and ready to take action when the course opens again.

Q: Once we wrap up our first launch, what do we do?

A: What you do after your launch is just as important, if not more important, than what you do during your launch. This is the time to build momentum for your next round. Start by showing up consistently for your audience. Keep doing weekly content like Facebook Lives, podcast episodes, blog posts, or emails. Be present, be helpful, and keep building that relationship. Your audience is paying attention and gaining value from what you’re teaching, even between launches, and when you open the doors again, they’ll be warmed up and ready to go.

In terms of your to-dos, make sure to check out The Post-Launch Checklist in Module 7, Lesson 7 of Your Ready to Launch Digital Course Track. It will walk you through how to properly close out your Live launch and prepare for the next one.

And if your first launch didn’t go as planned or didn’t meet your expectations, head to the Live Launch Troubleshooting Guide in the DCA Resources & Tech Library for practical next steps and strategies to turn lessons learned into future success. Every launch teaches you something that helps you grow and improve the next time around.

Q: What is the best way to grow your list between launches?

A: If you already have a solid foundation and a good-sized email list, this is your opportunity to scale using paid advertising. Facebook and Instagram ads are a powerful way to expand your reach and bring new people into your world quickly. I recommend mixing it up: run video ads, stack image ads, and definitely don’t skip Instagram Story ads. Story ads perform incredibly well when done right and can bring in a high volume of new leads.

You can also look into creating a quiz and running ads for your quiz. You can get thousands of people on your email list if you do a quiz right. For more resources, check out Interact; this is the software we use and recommend. You can also check out Chanti Zak, who is a quiz expert I rely on to create my quizzes!

Check out Module 5, List Building Bonus Lesson in Your Ready to Launch Digital Course Track for more help creating quizzes.

Q: Can I reduce my course price or change up my offer the second time I launch?

A: Yes, you can, but you’ll want to be strategic about how you do it. If you choose to lower the price the second time you launch, I recommend making a few clear changes to the offer itself. That way, previous students who paid more won’t feel like they missed out or overpaid.

For example, you might simplify the offer by removing a bonus or coaching element that was included the first time. Or you could position this new version as a “self-study” option if the first launch included more access or support.

You always want to honor the investment your first group of students made. That group helped you validate and shape your course, so if you're changing the pricing, make sure the new version looks and feels different enough that it doesn’t create confusion or frustration for your existing students.

Q: When you launch for the second time, do you do everything exactly the same?

A: No, and it shouldn’t be. I always look at my second launch (and every launch after that) as an opportunity to improve. After every Live launch, my team and I do a full debrief to talk through what worked well and what didn’t. We look at the numbers, talk about the content, revisit the offer, and review feedback from our audience. Then we set goals to do better next time, and we always aim to evolve and grow.

That doesn’t mean you need to overhaul your entire launch. In fact, I recommend focusing on just a few key areas to improve each time. Maybe your webinar content needs a refresh, or you want to test different sales page copy, or perhaps your email sequence could use stronger subject lines or calls to action. Small tweaks can lead to big results.

I use The Launch Revenue Formula Worksheet in Module 5, Lesson 2 of Your Ready to Launch Digital Course Track to guide my post-launch reflection. If your launch didn’t go as planned, be sure to check out The Live Launch Troubleshooting Checklist in the DCA Resources & Tech Library. It’s packed with insights to help you course correct for next time.

Remember, each launch is a chance to learn, grow, and better serve your audience. Aim to make progress, not perfection.
